An Orphan’s Story

After I lost my mother, every sound was a threat: a rustle in the grass of the savannah, a faraway pop, the screech of birds. I huddled under a tree, shaking uncontrollably. I was huge, yes, but I was still young and I had never been without my mother. Without her, I had no idea how to live. Without her, I was terrified.

When I had first seen the men running toward us with their weapons, I raised my trunk high in the air and blared a warning. But I was too late to save my mother.

My family and I could do nothing but scatter, hoping to survive. In a panic, I ran faster than I ever had, so fast the wind whooshed in my flapping ears. I ran farther than I ever meant to. When I finally stopped, everything was quiet, and my family was gone.

After days in my hiding place, I was parched. I walked into the hot sun, alert to noises, until I reached a water hole. I gulped water, barely taking a breath, and squirted big trunkfuls onto my back. I was so busy drinking that I didn't notice I had sunk deep into the mud -- this had happened to me once before, but my mother had pulled me out. Now, I was helpless. For days, I whimpered, heartbroken and hungry.

When a man found me in the mud, I tried to struggle, but I was too weak. This man seemed different from the ones I had seen before. He approached slowly, offered a hand, and then dripped a bottle into my mouth: milk, like my mother's. "Nice boy," he said. He spoke softly, and even through my exhaustion, I could sense that his gentleness was real.

Now that I am fully grown, I know that I was right to trust him. The man, David, became one of my keepers at the sanctuary, and the other animals he cared for became my new family. For years he and my other keepers fed me, took me on walks, played games with me, and soothed me when I woke from nightmares.

I will never forget the worst part of my life, but I will also never forget the kindness that saved me afterward.
