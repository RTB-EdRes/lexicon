The Gardener’s Roses

Long ago, in a little village, a gardener named Claude tended his roses. His gorgeous flowers blossomed like splendid, silky fireworks. The birds chirped in his trees, and butterflies fluttered about. All who strolled by marveled at the wondrous sight.

“Nowhere in the world will you find more magnificent roses,” Claude boasted to the passing villagers as he rested on his garden bench. “Truly, I’m the finest gardener in our village.”

The villagers quietly nodded. They could not argue with Claude’s claim, but they did tire of hearing it.

However, one morning, when Claude stepped into his garden, his eyes widened. All around him, ragged rose petals covered the ground. The birds were pecking at his flowers and ruining them.

Claude’s face reddened. With a furious shout, he waved his arms at the birds, and at last, all the feathered foes flew off.

Unfortunately, the birds returned the next morning, and torn flower petals littered the ground again. With an even fiercer determination, Claude drove out the birds. When he finished the task, he marched into his cottage to find his cat. He needed an attentive guard to protect his precious roses.

Two days passed peacefully. With the cat patrolling the garden, the birds finally stayed away. Like royalty, Claude paraded through his roses and congratulated himself. “Perhaps I am the finest gardener in the world,” he thought.

But on the third day, Claude entered his garden and his jaw dropped. His roses had withered and wilted. When Claude examined the plants, lacy holes peppered their leaves and petals. Thousands of tiny insects were feasting on them.

A wave of hopelessness flooded over Claude. Suddenly, he realized why the birds had been pecking at his flowers: they were devouring the pests that Claude’s eyes had missed.

Dismally, Claude retreated to his garden bench, surveying his tragic flowers. Alone, he could never win a battle against the countless insects. He soon realized his own foolishness. He had never appreciated his silent, feathered helpers or recognized their role.

Standing, Claude called for his cat and put his pet inside the cottage. Next, in the center of his garden, he began gathering a stack of stones.

“What are you building?” asked some curious villagers passing by.

“A birdbath,” announced Claude. “I must persuade my feathered friends to return. They are the expert gardeners!” he declared.
