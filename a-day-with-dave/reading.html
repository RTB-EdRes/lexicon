A Day with Dave

The phone rang. Celia figured it was probably one of her friends, so she jumped up and answered it.

"Hello, may I speak to Celia Cruz?" asked the voice on the telephone. Celia said she was speaking.

The voice said, "Hi Celia, I'm calling from Channel 5 News to let you know you're the winner of our Meteorologist for a Day drawing."

Celia had forgotten about the drawing she entered months earlier at the State Fair. The news station explained that she would go to the studio the following week for her prize. She would have lunch with Dave from the weather team, take a tour of the set, and do her own on-air weather segment. Celia couldn't wait to tell her friends she was going to be on TV.

For the rest of the week, Celia told everyone she knew to look for her on the 6:00 news. As word spread, Celia started to feel sort of famous. Her friends asked her to mention them or give them secret signals on the air. Celia enjoyed the fame. She didn't put much thought into being a meteorologist, or a scientist who studies the atmosphere. "How hard can it be to point at a weather map?" she bragged. She had watched countless meteorologists use maps to talk about weather in different places before.

Finally, Celia's prize day came. She took the bus to the TV studio and met Dave Patterson, the evening weatherman. The two of them had lunch. Then Dave took Celia around the set. "That's the news desk," he pointed, "and that's where we do makeup." It was all very interesting to Celia, but she wanted to hurry up and get on the air. Her friends were watching and waiting.

Dave told her, "Just read the temperatures off the monitor. Then point to the area of rain on the map. That's it."

But suddenly it didn't seem so easy. There were half a dozen cameras pointed at Celia. The weather map was just a green screen. The monitor was full of city names that Celia wasn't sure she could pronounce. "I don't think I can do this," she gulped, turning bright red. She looked like she might run off the set.

"How much time do we have?" asked Dave.

A producer shouted, "Five minutes."

Dave crouched down in front of Celia. "I know it's a lot to take in," he said. "Don't worry about the pressure." He told her, "Sometimes, I still get scared on camera. People get upset when I get the forecast wrong. But I give a big smile and try to bring those people some sunshine, whether it's raining or not."

"Thirty seconds!" yelled the producer. Dave winked at Celia and backed away. She took her place by the green screen.

After the show, Dave congratulated Celia. "I think you have a talent for being on camera," he said. "Maybe when you're a little older, you can get an internship here at the studio."

"Maybe I can go to school to become a meteorologist," said Celia. "I hope my friends were watching."
