Jennifer couldn’t believe it. “Ah, man!” she whined. “I’ve been trying to beat Karina since January. Why is she so fast?”

Jennifer’s friend Inez watched as Karina ran fifty yards in eight seconds. “I don’t know,” said Inez. “But that girl is like lightning.”

The girls grabbed their gear and headed back to the locker room. “What’s your goal this nine weeks?” asked Jennifer.

“I’m working on push-ups,” said Inez. “I want to be able to do fifty push-ups a day. And real ones, not the modified kind.”

“How’s it going?” Jennifer asked.

“Pretty good,” replied Inez. “I’m up to 35.”

“That’s great!” said Jennifer. “You’ll definitely meet your goal!”

The girls were in a special class at Blufton Elementary School. It was called Health and Society. The class met once a week to discuss things like eating right, exercising and helping others. Students set new personal goals every nine-week period.

“Well, I’m trying to beat Karina!,” Jennifer announced. “She’s the fastest girl in school and I know I can beat her if I keep trying.”

“Your goal is to be faster than Karina?” asked Inez.

“Yeah,” replied Jennifer. “Why do you ask it that way?”

“Well, Mr. Chavez says goals are supposed to be personal.”

“Yeah, I’m going to personally beat her!”

“You know what I mean,” said Inez. “Goals are supposed to be about making ourselves better, not comparing to others. How are you ever going to beat Karina? I heard her say that her goal is to run fifty yards in 7.6 seconds. What’s your time?”

“Well it was 9.2 in January. But now I’m down to 8.1.”

“You mean you cut a whole second off your fifty-yard time? That’s incredible! You should feel proud about that!”

“You’re right, Inez,” said Jennifer. “If I compare to Karina, I feel bad. When I think about how much faster I’ve gotten this month, I feel great. I’m going to talk to Mr. Chavez about resetting my goal.”

“That’s a great idea! Now come…”
